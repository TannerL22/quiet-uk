# Noise data and estimation research — 29 September 2026

## Decision

There are useful additional data sources, and prediction is scientifically possible. This search did not establish a ready-to-use, independently validated, UK-wide grid of total ambient sound that resolves the quiet end of the installed Oxford–London release.

Prioritise acquiring or clarifying original model evidence, then a bounded estimation experiment. A prediction must have its own provenance, uncertainty and evaluation; it must not overwrite provider values or turn every missing cell into quiet.

This is a research assessment and proposed experiment. No noise data was installed, no analytical values were changed, and no external contact was made.

## Why the current map has blanks

[Defra’s explanation](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/strategic-noise-mapping-2022/explaining-the-2022-noise-maps) says its road/rail outputs are modelled on a 10 m grid at 4 m height. Most indicators stop reporting below 40 dB; night stops below 35 dB. These maps already use acoustic prediction, rather than measurements at every cell.

Reporting limits plausibly explain some blank support. However, the project’s [encoding audit](PROVIDER_ENCODING_AND_EXTRACTION_AUDIT.md) found that road/rail numeric zeros, declared nodata, airport missingness and calculation-domain membership cannot all be interpreted identically. The published rectangle is not a valid-domain mask. Provider reporting cutoffs alone do not certify every zero or missing cell as below that cutoff.

The best first data request would be unthresholded receiver outputs, or the complete source/model package that can reproduce them, together with product-specific domain masks and encoding definitions. No publicly downloadable complete quiet-end package was established in this pass. Requesting it is a proposed next action, not an action already taken.

## Prediction methods and the enclosed-hole example

A blank enclosed by low reported values is useful spatial evidence. It is not a mathematical certificate of the interior level. The map colour is a bin for one source and indicator; a source inside the hole, shielding, terrain, changed geometry or a data-domain omission can invalidate a smooth interpolation assumption. A low-road prediction also says nothing by itself about aircraft or other sounds.

Three method families deserve comparison:

1. **Local interpolation:** nearest-neighbour/IDW as simple baselines, followed by kriging or Gaussian-process interpolation. Restrict to small gaps within a consistent source/period/domain; account for roads, barriers and hole size. [A primary interpolation study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5087480/) tests kriging and a road-aware method using cross-validation, demonstrating that interpolation is an evaluated modelling choice rather than a universal guarantee.
2. **Censor-aware spatial regression:** use exact nearby values, distance and source activity, terrain/buildings and land cover. Use a below-threshold likelihood only when the censoring meaning is justified; retain ordinary missing values separately. [Staab et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41370-021-00355-z) demonstrate filling road-noise map gaps with land-use regression and spatial cross-validation. Their virtual campaigns use a simulation reference, so published performance does not establish accuracy for real English below-cutoff cells.
3. **Acoustic propagation:** calculate source contributions with physically appropriate models. [NoiseModelling](https://noise-planet.org/noisemodelling.html) describes direct, reflected and diffracted paths and energetic summation. The [2025 Defra-system paper](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2025/data/articles/000669.pdf) identifies extensive traffic, surface, rail, ground and meteorological input work. A proper model is possible; its accuracy depends on those inputs.

Prediction confidence must depend on source completeness, distance from supported observations, model domain and validation performance. A range or calibrated probability of falling below a threshold is often more defensible than a finely rounded point estimate. If authoritative evidence establishes a cell is below a reporting threshold, show that bound directly; interpolation is unnecessary to invent an exact value.

## Existing project evidence

The most recent [Phase 2C experiment](PHASE2C_ROAD_SOURCE_INTEGRITY.md) used ten-region spatial holdouts. Its complete road model documented RMSE of **6.969 dB for Lden** and **7.779 dB for Lnight** against reported provider cells. Quiet-boundary rankings were weak. More than 90% of road links used a global traffic fallback in that experiment; observed speed coverage was absent, and important propagation effects were omitted.

These are model-agreement results, not independent measurement accuracy, and cannot validate absolute levels where the reference has no numeric value. The older prototypes also made censoring assumptions that the later provider audit does not authorise universally. Reusing their algorithms requires a new interpretation contract rather than inheriting those assumptions.

## Proposed bounded experiment

1. Select representative road gaps within the installed release. Inventory zero/nodata/domain-edge states and connected-component size, nearby values, road geometry and shielding evidence. Keep inferred causes explicitly provisional.
2. For interpolation feasibility, hide entire known patches with geometry and sizes resembling real gaps. Compare simple baselines, spatial regression and an acoustic model where inputs permit. Hold out geographic areas and buffer train/test splits; neighbouring pixels must not make transferability look artificially strong.
3. Do not call this a quiet-end validation. Real threshold-selected blanks differ from randomly removed reported cells. Obtain authoritative unthresholded reference outputs and/or calibrated, source/period-matched measurements below the cutoff; design adequate temporal sampling for annual claims.
4. Predefine acceptable error, bias, interval coverage and false-quiet rates for the intended use. Report results by gap size, source proximity, terrain and urban/rural context. An RMSE target or interval coverage target is a proposed acceptance criterion, not a regulatory standard. Abstain outside demonstrated support.
5. If evaluation succeeds, publish a separate versioned estimate layer. Preserve provider values. In the interface distinguish reported model values, verified below-reporting bounds, estimates with uncertainty, and unavailable evidence. Combine sources only after period/metric/support compatibility and missing contributions are addressed.

For the product, the useful near-term target is a trustworthy classification or range for selected gaps, followed by better absolute estimates as evidence permits. Do not promise property-level total sound from colour interpolation.

## Search record and limits

Reviewed official England, Welsh, Scottish and Northern Irish mapping material; airport/CAA publications; Natural England’s 2026 indicator and methodology; soundscape and crowdsourced datasets; propagation tools and primary interpolation/regression studies. Dataset pages and selected technical reports were inspected; large source rasters and restricted files were not acquired, and no new model was fitted.

Firecrawl search/research skills were consulted, but the connected search account returned HTTP 402. Research continued with direct web search and primary-page retrieval. Some publisher pages intermittently failed; the land-use regression paper’s content was available through indexed publisher search. No claim is made that absence from this search proves a dataset does not exist.

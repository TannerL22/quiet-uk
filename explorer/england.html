      About this map

      These are Defra's official strategic noise maps for England, made with a computer model for the year 2021. They show the average level over a whole year, 4 metres above the ground. They are not live sound and not measurements.

      • Darker means louder. Every 10 dB step sounds roughly twice as loud.
      • Each source is separate. Road, rail and aircraft are mapped one at a time and are not added together.
      • “Under 40 dB” (under 35 at night) means Defra's model worked out a level below that, but the exact number isn't published.
      • Grey stripes or blank mean no published value. That is not the same as quiet.
      • Aircraft noise is only mapped around major airports.

        How to read this map

        The colours show how much transport noise reaches each 10 metre square, as a yearly average. Defra worked these out with a computer model for 2021, 4 metres above the ground (about first-floor window height).

        The colours

        The words are for the 24-hour and daytime averages. At night they apply 10 dB lower (so 45 dB at night counts as “Noticeable”, like 55 dB in the day), matching the extra weight the 24-hour measure gives to night noise.

        Decibels in plain terms

        • Noise is measured in decibels (dB). The scale isn't linear: every 10 dB sounds about twice as loud, and a 3 dB change is about the smallest most people notice.
        • These are averages over a year. A quiet street with a few loud lorries can have the same average as a steady hum.
        • The World Health Organization recommends keeping average road noise below 53 dB (24-hour) and 45 dB at night. The map tells you when a spot is above that.

        The three time options

        • 24-hour (Lden): the yearly average over the whole day, with evening noise counted 5 dB louder and night noise 10 dB louder, because noise bothers people more then.
        • Daytime (Lday): the average from 7am to 7pm.
        • Night (Lnight): the average from 11pm to 7am.

        What the map can't tell you

        • Each source is separate. Road, rail and aircraft are never added together here.
        • Below 40 dB (35 at night), Defra doesn't publish the exact level, so those places all look the same.
        • No published value is not silence. Grey stripes mean Defra published nothing for that square.
        • Other sounds, such as people, industry, wind or birds, aren't included. Neither are single loud events.
        • A 10 metre square is how finely the map is drawn, not a promise of 10 metre accuracy.
